IAST Calculator

User Guide

Overview

This app predicts multicomponent adsorption equilibria using Ideal Adsorbed Solution Theory (IAST). Single-component isotherm data exported from the Sorption Isotherm Fitting app are loaded for each component; IAST then computes the mixture loadings and selectivities at any specified bulk-phase composition and pressure.

Loading components

Drop one or more isotherm CSV files (exported from the Isotherm Fitting app) anywhere on the app — each becomes its own component. The app reads the model name and fitted parameters automatically. If no Sorptive row is found in the CSV, select the gas manually (or choose Custom) in the component card.

Drag a component card by its grip handle (⣿) to reorder the components. Order matters: in "Sweep bulk mole fraction" mode, the swept fraction y₁ always refers to whichever component is first in the list, so reordering changes which gas that sweep and the "Component loadings vs y₁" plot refer to. Reordering clears any existing results, so re-run IAST afterwards.

Calculation modes

Sweep total pressure: Fix the bulk mole fractions yi and compute IAST results across a pressure range. Good for adsorption isotherms and selectivity vs pressure plots.

Sweep bulk mole fraction: Fix total pressure and sweep y₁ from ~0 to ~1. Good for adsorption isotherms and selectivity as a function of bulk-phase composition.

Output types

Plots are shown automatically based on the calculation mode. Sweep total pressure shows three plots: component loadings (including total) vs pressure, adsorbed-phase mole fractions vs pressure, and selectivity vs pressure. Sweep bulk mole fraction shows two plots: component loadings vs y₁ and selectivity vs y₁.

IAST theory

IAST (Myers & Prausnitz, 1965) extends Raoult's law to adsorption. At equilibrium, the spreading pressure of each component in the mixture equals a common spreading pressure Π. The spreading pressure is defined as Π = (RT/A)∫₀P°ᵢ qᵢ(P)/P dP where A is the specific surface area. IAST solves for the hypothetical single-component pressures P°ᵢ such that all Πᵢ are equal, then computes the adsorbed-phase loading from the mole fractions xᵢ = yᵢP/P°ᵢ (normalised) and total loading 1/q_total = Σxᵢ/qᵢ(P°ᵢ).

Selectivity

Adsorption selectivity of component i over j is defined as Sij = (xi/xj) / (yi/yj), where x and y are adsorbed- and gas-phase mole fractions respectively.
