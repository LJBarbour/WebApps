Method

The fitting range and surface area reported here are determined by the BET Surface Identification (BETSI) algorithm. BETSI applies a comprehensive, exhaustive search over all valid consecutive-point windows in the adsorption isotherm, evaluating each candidate range against all four Rouquerol consistency criteria and selecting the optimal result objectively, without manual intervention.

This approach was introduced and validated by a round-robin study involving 60 laboratories, demonstrating that conventional manual fitting of BET isotherms produces substantial inter-laboratory variation. The BETSI algorithm eliminates this ambiguity.

BET Surface Area — Theory & Method
The BET Equation

The Brunauer–Emmett–Teller (BET) theory1 models multilayer gas adsorption on a surface. Its linearised form is:

p/p₀ / [n(1 − p/p₀)]  =  1/(nₘC)  +  (C−1)/(nₘC) · p/p₀

A plot of the left-hand side against p/p₀ yields a straight line. From the slope s and intercept i:

  • nₘ = 1/(s + i) — monolayer capacity
  • C = s/i + 1 — BET energy constant
  • SBET = nₘ · NA · σ — specific surface area (m² g−¹)

where NA is Avogadro’s number and σ is the molecular cross-sectional area of the sorptive. The BET area is recognised by IUPAC as the standard metric for surface area characterisation2 and is an ISO standard (ISO 9277) since 1995.

The Rouquerol Consistency Criteria

Because the BET equation was originally developed for open surfaces, applying it to microporous materials requires careful selection of the fitting pressure range. Rouquerol et al.3 defined four criteria that any valid BET range must satisfy:

  1. C1a — n(1 − p/p₀) must increase monotonically with p/p₀ within the range.
  2. C2 — The BET constant C must be positive (ensures physical meaning of the monolayer loading).
  3. C3 — The pressure corresponding to monolayer loading must lie within the selected fitting range.
  4. C4 — The monolayer loading derived from the isotherm at that pressure must agree closely with the BET-derived value.

Manual application of these criteria is cumbersome in practice and leads to different researchers selecting different pressure ranges from identical isotherms. This variability is the central problem addressed by the BETSI algorithm.

The Reproducibility Problem

Osterrieth et al.4 conducted a major round-robin study in which 60 expert laboratories each calculated BET surface areas from the same 18 pre-measured isotherms. The results revealed dramatic inter-laboratory variation: for some materials the highest reported value exceeded the lowest by a factor of five or more. For example, the MOF NU-1104 returned estimates ranging from 1,757 to 9,341 m² g−¹ — a spread of 7,584 m² g−¹. Even for well-studied materials such as HKUST-1 and ZIF-8, no two laboratories reported identical values.

The study established two root causes:

  • The Rouquerol criteria are indeterminate — multiple pressure windows satisfy all four simultaneously, each returning a different BET area.
  • Manual implementation is inconsistent — researchers apply the criteria with different degrees of rigour, and commercial software packages offer varying levels of automation.

The conclusion is stark: without a standardised, automated procedure, BET areas reported in the literature cannot be reliably compared across research groups or instruments.

The BETSI Algorithm

To address this, Osterrieth et al. developed the BET Surface Identification (BETSI) algorithm, which removes all subjectivity from the range-selection step. The key features are:

Exhaustive search. Rather than selecting a single range manually, BETSI tests every consecutive sub-sequence of data points of at least the minimum length against all four Rouquerol criteria. For an isotherm with n points this evaluates on the order of n²/2 candidate windows.

PCHIP interpolation. Criteria C3 and C4 require reading the monolayer pressure from the actual isotherm. BETSI uses Piecewise Cubic Hermite Interpolating Polynomial (PCHIP) interpolation — a shape-preserving scheme that avoids the spurious oscillations of standard cubic splines — to reconstruct the isotherm continuously. The pressure p* at which the interpolated isotherm equals nₘ is found by bisection.

Pressure-domain C4 error. The criterion-4 agreement is expressed as a percentage error in the pressure domain: |p* − pcalc| / p* × 100, where pcalc = 1/(√C + 1) is the theoretical BET pressure at monolayer coverage. This is more physically meaningful than an uptake-domain comparison.

Extended criterion C1b. BETSI additionally requires the linearised BET function (the left-hand side of the BET equation) to have a positive regression slope — an extended form of the monotonicity requirement.

Isotherm-knee selection. From all windows that pass all criteria, BETSI identifies the isotherm knee: the subset of windows whose upper pressure boundary is the highest permissible pressure under criterion C1a. This knee marks the onset of multilayer adsorption. The optimal result is then the knee-group member with the lowest C4 percentage error, making the final selection unique and reproducible.

When the algorithm is applied to the 18 isotherms from the round-robin study, the spread of valid BETSI results is substantially narrower than that from manual calculation in every case — in some instances by more than an order of magnitude.

How This App Applies the Method

This application implements the BETSI algorithm in full, faithfully following the description in Osterrieth et al. (2022):

  • All consecutive-point windows of at least the minimum length are evaluated against criteria C1a, C1b, C2, C3, C4, and the R² threshold.
  • Criteria C3 and C4 are computed using PCHIP interpolation and bisection, exactly as in the original Python implementation.
  • The isotherm knee is identified and the optimal fit selected by minimum C4 error within the knee group.
  • The Analysis Plots tab reproduces three of the BETSI output figures: the scatter plot of all valid BET areas against their C4 error (with the knee group and optimal result highlighted), the distribution of valid BET areas, and the monolayer loading diagram on the isotherm.
  • The Isotherm, Rouquerol, and BET Linear tabs show the standard plots with the BETSI optimal range highlighted.

The Analysis Settings panel (right sidebar) exposes the three adjustable parameters: minimum points per fit, the R² threshold, and the maximum permitted C4 error. The defaults (10 points, R² ≥ 0.99, C4 ≤ 20%) correspond to those used in the original BETSI paper and software. Any change to these settings, or to the sorptive, sample mass, or uptake units, triggers an immediate re-analysis.

Note on limitations. BET theory assumes flat, homogeneous surfaces and multilayer adsorption. IUPAC advises that BET areas for microporous materials represent an “apparent surface area” and should be treated as a reproducible fingerprint rather than a true geometric surface area. The BETSI method reduces calculation variability but does not alter the underlying assumptions of the BET model.
