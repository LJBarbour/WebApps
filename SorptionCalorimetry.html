Sorption Calorimetry Viewer

User Guide

This app processes heat flow and pressure data from Setaram/BV sorption calorimetry instruments. It handles the pressure signal reconstruction required when a gauge-type pressure transmitter is used, and produces publication-quality heat flow vs pressure plots with integrated peak areas.

Instrument background

In a sorption calorimetry experiment, the sample cell pressure is controlled by a ProportionAir pressure valve (0–10 bar) and a syringe pump (10 bar upward). The calorimeter reads only the transmitter near the sample cell. Two types of transmitter are used:

Absolute transmitter (experiments below ~40 bar): reads correctly from 0 bar upward. No pressure reconstruction is needed.

Gauge transmitter (experiments above ~40 bar): has a lower floor, typically around 0.9–1.1 bar. Below this floor, the transmitter saturates and reports a constant value equal to the floor. It cannot read the 0–floor bar region, which must be reconstructed by extrapolation (see Pressure Interpolation below).

The experiment proceeds as follows: the system equilibrates at vacuum (pressure ≈ 0 bar) while the calorimeter records data. Once a stable heat flow baseline is achieved, the user starts the pressure ramp using the pressure valve. When the valve reaches its limit (~10 bar), the user manually switches to the syringe pump to reach higher pressures. At the peak pressure, the syringe pump ramps back down to ~10 bar, then the user switches back to the pressure valve to return to vacuum. Any delay during the valve/pump changeovers results in a flat pressure hold that is detected and removed by the app.

File format

Drop a tab-delimited .txt file onto the drop zone, or click the drop zone to browse. The file must contain a short metadata header followed by a data table with the header row Index  Time (s)  Temperature (°C)  HeatFlow (mW/g)  Analog signal (bar). The pressure unit is read from this header line. Metadata fields parsed from the header: Creation Date, User, Initial Mass (mg), and Molar Mass (g/mol).

Raw Data tab

Shows heat flow (left axis) and raw pressure (right axis) versus time. The traces are colour-coded by region:

■ Orange / blue — kept: data in the active ramp window (t₀ to t_end), excluding holds.

■ Amber / green — interpolated P region: data between t₀ and the transmitter wake point (adsorption) or between the transmitter sleep point and t_end (desorption). Heat flow here is real measured data; pressure is reconstructed by extrapolation.

■ Grey — omitted: pre-ramp baseline, mid-experiment valve/pump hold regions, and post-experiment baseline.

Use the Subsample control to reduce the number of plotted points for display performance — the full dataset is always used for analysis. The Desorption tail extension slider extends the data retention window beyond t_end (the extrapolated P=0 point) to capture slow post-desorption kinetics.

Pressure Interpolation tab

This tab shows the full pressure correction applied to the gauge transmitter data, with fit statistics.

Transmitter floor (Tx floor): the pressure value the transmitter reports when the true pressure is below its range. Detected automatically as the median of the first 500 data points (the pre-ramp baseline). You can override this with the Tx floor field.

Wake threshold: the transmitter is considered to have "woken up" (i.e. to be reporting reliable values) when the raw pressure exceeds Tx floor + 0.1 bar. This 0.1 bar safety margin is hardcoded to avoid using noisy readings near the floor. If your transmitter floor is, say, 2.0 bar, the interpolated region extends from 0 to approximately 2.1 bar, and the fit data begins at 2.1 bar.

Fit from / Fit to: the pressure range used for the linear regression of P vs t on each leg. Default 1.5–8.5 bar. The fit should be performed in the linear ramp region, avoiding the transmitter floor transition (lower end) and the syringe pump plateau or pressure overshoot (upper end). After changing these values, click Recalculate.

Pressure reconstruction: a straight line P = m·t + b is fitted through the [Fit from, Fit to] region on each leg. This line is extrapolated back to find t₀ (where P = 0 on adsorption) and t_end (where P = 0 on desorption). Points between t₀ and the wake threshold use the extrapolated line as their corrected pressure. Points above the wake threshold use the raw transmitter reading. The fit slope gives the ramp rate in mbar/min.

Interpolation Detail tab

Shows a zoomed view of the pressure reconstruction for adsorption (left) and desorption (right), from the start of the fit range down to P = 0. Each plot shows:

■ Grey line: raw pressure from the transmitter.

■ Coloured dots: points in the [Fit from, Fit to] range used for the linear regression.

■ Green dots/line: the interpolated region — points below the wake threshold (Tx floor + 0.1 bar) where pressure is reconstructed.

■ Black solid line: the linear fit over the [Fit from, Fit to] range, with equation and R² in the legend.

■ Orange dashed line: the extrapolation of the fit below Fit from down to P = 0.

Flat-segment detection

Flat pressure segments are detected using a rolling variance window of ±400 data points (~200 s at 0.5 s/point). Any region where the local variance stays below 0.005 bar² for at least 200 consecutive points is classified as flat and removed from the analysis and export. Three types of flat segment are removed:

1. Pre-ramp baseline: the equilibration period before the pressure ramp starts (before t₀).

2. Mid-experiment hold: the flat plateau that occurs when the user is not present to switch between the pressure valve and syringe pump (typically at ~10 bar).

3. Post-experiment baseline: the period after the pressure has returned to vacuum.

Note: the interpolated P regions (0 to wake threshold on each leg) are never removed even if their pressure variance is low, because the heat flow data there is real and scientifically important.

Heat Flow vs Pressure tab

Plots heat flow (mW/g) against the corrected pressure for the adsorption and/or desorption legs. Use the toggle buttons in the sidebar to show or hide each leg. The Remove holds toggle excludes flat-segment data points from the plot. The right-hand control panel provides full publication-quality styling controls: axis ranges, labels and fonts, grid and tick marks, line colours and styles, legend position, chart title, and image export (PNG or JPEG at any resolution).

Zoom: click and drag to rubber-band zoom, scroll wheel to zoom in/out centred on the cursor, double-click to reset zoom. The ⟲ Reset button in the top-right corner of the chart also resets zoom.

Running integral

Enable in the Integration panel (right-hand controls). Displays the cumulative integral of heat flow with respect to time as a dashed overlay on the Heat Flow vs Pressure chart. Time is not directly available in the P-space plot, but is reconstructed from the pressure ramp rate: dt = |dP / ramp rate|, where the ramp rate (bar/s) comes from the linear fit on each leg.

The adsorption integral accumulates from low P to high P (upward on screen). The desorption integral is vertically mirrored — it accumulates from high P to low P and is displayed inverted, so both integrals visually resemble sorption isotherms. Each leg is independently scaled to fill the full plot height. The integral units are mJ/g (mW/g × s).

Peak area integration

Click Pick two points in the Integration panel, then click two points on the Heat Flow vs Pressure chart to bracket a peak. A straight-line baseline is drawn between the two clicked points; the area between the signal and the baseline is integrated. The result is reported in mJ/g, using the ramp rate to convert pressure intervals to time: ∫ (HF − baseline) · dt where dt = |dP / ramp rate|. If the sample mass is available from the file header, the total energy in mJ is also reported.

For the low-pressure peak at the start of adsorption or end of desorption: click the first point near or below P = 0 (the left edge of the plot). If the click is below the lowest pressure data point, the baseline anchor is automatically set to (P = 0, HF = 0). Click the second point on the other side of the peak. Up to one adsorption and one desorption integration can be shown simultaneously. Click Clear all to remove both.

CSV export

The Export CSV button exports the processed data with flat segments removed and the time axis adjusted to reflect only active ramp time. Columns: adjusted time (s), corrected pressure (bar or mbar), heat flow (mW/g), leg (adsorption/desorption). The Export Raw CSV button exports the full raw dataset without any processing. In Chrome and Edge, both buttons open a native Save As dialog so you can choose the filename and folder directly.
