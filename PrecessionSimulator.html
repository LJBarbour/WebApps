Precession Simulator guide

The program draws a simulated precession photograph of one layer of reciprocal space, using only the reflections that are actually in your data.

Loading data

  • Drag files onto the window (several at once is fine), or use Open files. Accepted: .ins, .res, .hkl, .cif (and .fcf).
  • .ins / .res give the unit cell (from the CELL card), which scales the reciprocal axes and angles, and the wavelength (used only for 2θ).
  • .hkl gives the reflections: h k l, intensity (F²) and σ, in the SHELX fixed format or separated by spaces. Reading stops at a 0 0 0 line. A browser cannot look for the .hkl next to the .ins, so drop both.
  • .cif gives both. Reflections are taken from a _refln_ loop with _refln_F_squared_meas (or _refln_F_meas), a _diffrn_refln_ loop with _diffrn_refln_intensity_net, or an embedded _shelx_hkl_file. Calculated-only structure factors are not used.
  • If the cell or the reflections are missing, the film area says what is missing. Dropping a new file of one kind replaces the earlier one of that kind.
  • Reflections with the same h k l are averaged. Negative intensities are shown as zero.

Viewing

  • View down a*, b* or c* selects the layer type: down a* shows 0kl-type layers (h fixed), down b* h0l-type (k fixed), down c* hk0-type (l fixed). Axes are drawn right-handed: down c*, a* points right and b* up; down a*, b* right and c* up; down b*, c* right and a* up.
  • Roll the mouse wheel over the picture (or use the arrow keys, Page Up/Down, or the number box) to move up and down the layers.
  • True layer offset: in a real precession photograph the axis passes through the point of the layer that lies on the zone axis. When the cell is oblique the origin of an upper layer is therefore displaced from the film centre. Untick the box to put every layer's origin at the centre.
  • Film radius zooms in by shrinking the circle (the film is limited to a circle of reciprocal space; the readout gives the resolution at its edge).

Reciprocal lattice (3D tab)

  • The second tab shows the reciprocal lattice itself, with one sphere for each measured reflection. The sphere radius follows the intensity in the same way as the Spot radius mode of the photograph (the Spot intensity slider acts on both; Spot size sets the largest sphere as a percentage of the shortest reciprocal axis).
  • Drag with the left button to rotate, hold the right button and drag to zoom. The mouse wheel steps through the layers, exactly as on the photograph tab. Double-click, or press Align with photo, to look at the lattice exactly as the photograph does (same axis up and right). The view centre and scale stay fixed while you rotate; only the shading with depth changes.
  • The layer shown in the photograph is highlighted in orange, with the other reflections dimmed, and a translucent disc marks the plane of that layer. Step through the layers (number box, arrow keys) to see the plane move. Tick Show only current layer to hide everything else.
  • Reciprocal axes are drawn from the origin (the small dot, where the 000 reflection would be). Hovering over a sphere gives the same readout as the photograph. Friedel pairs, Reverse colours and Reciprocal axes apply to both tabs.

The third tab, Both side by side, shows the photograph on the left and the 3D lattice on the right. They share the layer, so the highlighted plane in the 3D view is always the one in the photograph; every control works as on the separate tabs.

Appearance

  • Grey shading: every spot is the same size and its brightness shows the intensity. Spot radius: every spot is pure white (or black when reversed) and its radius shows the intensity.
  • Reverse colours swaps white spots on a black film for dark spots on pale film.
  • Spot intensity sets how strong the spots are over the film background: raise it to bring up weak reflections. The scale is relative to the strong reflections of the whole data set (99.5th percentile), so it stays the same from layer to layer. Film background sets the fog level of the film. Spot size is a percentage of the spacing between lattice rows (diameter in grey mode, largest diameter in radius mode).
  • Generate Friedel pairs: where h k l is missing but −h −k −l was measured, the intensity of the measured one is used. Nothing else is filled in; all other missing reflections stay missing. Symmetry equivalents other than Friedel pairs are never generated.
  • Reciprocal axes are solid lines through the origin of the layer; indexing lines (dashed) run through the rows and columns of the lattice.
  • Hover over the picture for h k l, d, 2θ, I, σ and relative intensity (relative to the strongest reflection). It also tells you when a lattice position has no data.
